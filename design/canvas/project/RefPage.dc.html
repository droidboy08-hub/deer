Float glass

Flat sheet glass made by floating molten glass on a bath of molten tin.

Process

Molten soda-lime glass flows from the furnace onto a bath of molten tin, under an atmosphere of nitrogen and hydrogen that keeps the tin from oxidizing. The glass spreads into a ribbon of even thickness with fire-polished surfaces on both sides, then passes through an annealing lehr and is cut to size.

History

Developed at Pilkington in the United Kingdom by Alastair Pilkington and Kenneth Bickerstaff during the 1950s, the process was licensed to glassmakers around the world and largely replaced ground and polished plate glass.

Properties

Both faces come out flat and parallel without grinding. The side that rested on the tin carries a trace of it, which glaziers find with an ultraviolet lamp before coating or toughening a sheet.

Uses

Almost all flat glass for windows, mirrors, shopfronts and car glazing starts as float glass. Sheets are often toughened, laminated or coated after they leave the line.
